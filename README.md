## Project Overview

This repository documents a Cell and Molecular Biology laboratory investigation of the molecular relationship between DNA mutation, protein sequence change, cellular function, and disease phenotype using the LRRK2 G2019S mutation associated with familial Parkinson disease.

The project includes the wild-type LRRK2 coding sequence and predicted protein, the documented G2019S mutant, a student-created artificial mutation, protein-sequence comparisons, molecular interpretation, and the final laboratory report.

## Disease Background

LRRK2-related familial Parkinson disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that primarily affects movement. Major clinical manifestations include bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity, gait disturbance, and postural instability. It is commonly inherited in an autosomal dominant manner with reduced and age-dependent penetrance.

The disease is associated with pathogenic variants in the LRRK2 gene. In this project, the documented variant investigated was NM_198578.4:c.6055G>A, which produces the amino-acid substitution p.Gly2019Ser (G2019S).

## Normal Gene and Protein Function

LRRK2 encodes leucine-rich repeat serine/threonine-protein kinase 2, a large multidomain protein with kinase and GTPase-related functions. It participates in intracellular signaling, Rab-mediated membrane trafficking, vesicle transport, endolysosomal regulation, autophagy-related processes, and cytoskeletal function.

LRRK2 is mainly cytoplasmic but can associate with intracellular membranes and organelles. The G2019 residue is located within the kinase domain of the protein.
